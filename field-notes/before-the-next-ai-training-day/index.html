Before the Next AI Training Day.

Training day is the easy part. The real test is a Tuesday three weeks later, when a teacher has twenty minutes, a stack of grading and nobody from the session in the building.

From the field

Eighty educators, one question.

This October I spent a day training about 80 teachers and administrators from a group of Catholic K-8 schools, part of a year-long professional development program.

A training day can go well and still leave the hard part for later. The question I care about is what happens on that Tuesday in November. So this issue is about that Tuesday.

One practical signal

Leave with a task, in your own words, with a day attached.

A named task with a day attached gets tried. A general idea gets admired and shelved. Which of these would you actually do on Tuesday?

Task A: "Use AI for lesson planning."
Task B: "Draft three reading questions at two levels for Thursday's chapter, then fix whatever's wrong with them."
Pick one.

If you lead PD, collect those tasks on the way out. Two weeks later, ask how they went. The answers will tell you more than any exit survey.

One small thing to think about

A tour of tools ages fast. A habit lasts.

Most AI training for teachers is a tour of tools, and tools change every few months. Habits outlast them: checking what the AI got wrong, deciding what a student should still do by hand, knowing what never gets pasted in.

Our job is preparing students for their future, not our past. That starts with the adults in the building practicing habits that will outlast the next round of tools.
